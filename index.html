How flags are set (thresholds)

FIRST WARN WEATHER DAY (potentially dangerous; supersedes First Warn): SPC Day 1–3 categorical Slight (2/5)+ over KC · SPC Day 4–8 ≥15% area over KC · snow ≥1" at KCI (NWS gridpoint snowfall, model-median best-fit snow from the winter section, or winter event guidance median) · freezing-rain ice ≥0.01" (NWS gridpoint or model median) · wind chill ≤-10°F or heat index ≥105°F (NWS gridpoint) · any NWS warning (except river/creek Flood Warnings, shown as notes only, and Freeze Warnings, which are First Warn only for plant impacts), flood/flash-flood/severe/winter-storm watch, winter-weather/cold/heat advisory for a DMA point.

FIRST WARN (disruptive, not necessarily dangerous): daytime (6 AM–8 PM CT) NWS gridpoint hourly PoP >50% (beyond the NWS grid or if NWS fails: >50% of models with ≥0.01" daytime precip; commute windows 6–9 AM / 4–7 PM noted) · dense fog: NWS gridpoint visibility ≤0.25 mi or Dense Fog Advisory (model visibility is not in our model feed) · gusts ≥40 mph (NWS gridpoint or model-median peak) · high or daytime feels-like drops ≥15°F day to day (day 1 vs yesterday’s observed at KCI; feels-like = 7 AM–7 PM average) · light wintry mix without accumulation (NWS weather likely+, or ≥half the models) · Wind/Frost/Dense Smoke advisories · Freeze Warning (sensitive plants; not a Weather Day).

.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 233 AM CST Fri Nov 13 2026

Water vapor satellite imagery shows a trough ridge trough pattern 
east to west across the central CONUS. The sinusoidal nature of the 
overall flow is a broad contributor to the rollercoaster ride of 
temperatures that are expected through the period. Northerly flow 
continues to dominate the short term forecast with cool Canadian air 
advecting into the region. High temperatures only look to reach the 
upper 40s across the area. Points near La Cygne and Butler may touch 
50F. As the surface high continues to sag southwards, winds look to 
keep conditions a bit brisk, but nowhere near the extend we saw the 
past 2 days (although temperatures were much warmer).

The peak of this cool stretch comes tonight into Saturday morning as 
the center of the surface high slowly migrated through southern IA. 
Light surface winds as well as a combination of cold air advection 
aloft (limiting an inversion) and clear skies allow strong nocturnal 
cooling which lowers overnight temperatures to the 20s with at 20-30 
percent chance that portions of central and northeast MO could dip 
into the upper teens.

Sub freezing lows continue through much of the weekend with portions 
of central and eastern MO seeing lows < 32F through Tuesday morning. 
Late in the weekend, the trough currently situated across the 
western CONUS begins to migrate eastward. As it does so, a lee 
trough at low levels advects some warmer air from the southern CONUS 
northward pushing temperatures back upward to the 50s. Midweek a 
subsequent trough and preceding ridge enter the central CONUS. Flow 
opens up from the desert SW and the Gulf which when combined with 
the tighten pressure gradient between these systems vaults warm air 
northward. The result is another period of warmer than seasonal 
temperatures with highs for the tail end of next week reaching the 
60s. If we flip the parameters of the forecast temperature ensembles 
around they show a 10-20 percent chance of temperatures exceeding 
70F which may not be a lot, but is more than one would expect this 
late into November.

The warm temperatures next week are welcome as that looks to be the 
next opportunity for precipitation across the region. Extended 
guidance hints at a fairly interesting situation developing 
particularly in the midlevel where an expansive high across the 
eastern CONUS and a low across the west create a tight pressure 
gradient across the intermountain west. This compression of warm air 
and moisture flow combined with the exit region of an upper level 
trough shows the potential for the development of and expansive area 
of precipitation across the central CONUS Friday. However, much of 
the activity looks to remain west of our area. Additional ejecting 
shortwaves from the trough through next weekend bring opportunities 
for showers and maybe a few thunderstorms. Of course, several 
uncertainties remain and guidance does tend to spatially broad brush 
PoPs the further out in time they run. We will have to see how the 
pattern evolves over the next several days.
