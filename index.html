How flags are set (thresholds)

FIRST WARN WEATHER DAY (potentially dangerous; supersedes First Warn): SPC Day 1–3 categorical Slight (2/5)+ over KC · SPC Day 4–8 ≥15% area over KC · snow ≥1" at KCI (NWS gridpoint snowfall, model-median best-fit snow from the winter section, or winter event guidance median) · freezing-rain ice ≥0.01" (NWS gridpoint or model median) · wind chill ≤-10°F or heat index ≥105°F (NWS gridpoint) · any NWS warning (except river/creek Flood Warnings, shown as notes only, and Freeze Warnings, which are First Warn only for plant impacts), flood/flash-flood/severe/winter-storm watch, winter-weather/cold/heat advisory for a DMA point.

FIRST WARN (disruptive, not necessarily dangerous): daytime (6 AM–8 PM CT) NWS gridpoint hourly PoP >50% (beyond the NWS grid or if NWS fails: >50% of models with ≥0.01" daytime precip; commute windows 6–9 AM / 4–7 PM noted) · dense fog: NWS gridpoint visibility ≤0.25 mi or Dense Fog Advisory (model visibility is not in our model feed) · gusts ≥40 mph (NWS gridpoint or model-median peak) · high drops ≥20°F day to day · light wintry mix without accumulation (NWS weather likely+, or ≥half the models) · Wind/Frost/Dense Smoke advisories · Freeze Warning (sensitive plants; not a Weather Day).

.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 136 PM CST Mon Nov 9 2026

Mid and upper-level synoptic pattern is fairly progressive for the 
northern third of the CONUS and into the Canadian Prairie Provinces. 
This is currently resulting in modest mid-level ridging across the 
eastern Plains and lower Missouri River Valley this afternoon, 
continuing the development of the surface anticyclone, but is 
starting to push southeastward. This will continue to keep skies 
mostly clear this evening. We will need to monitor fog trends this 
evening and overnight, but if winds hold between 3 and 5 kts, its 
development may be inhibited. Low-lying areas sheltered from the 
wind and river valleys may still fog in this evening. Tuesday into 
Wednesday morning, H5 short-wave and associated vort maxima travels 
out of the Rockies and de-amplifies the mid-level ridge, with dCVA 
developing a surface cyclone in the northern High Plains. Ahead of 
this lower-level flow turns more southwesterly, with increased WAA 
from the Central Plains toward the lower Missouri River Valley. This 
will send temperatures into the mid 60s for most of the forecast 
area, with areas further south of Interstate 70 potentially hitting 
the lower 70s. As the attendant cyclone continues to deepen, strong 
cold front will trail this system, and increase convergence heading 
into Wednesday. 

The rate of H5 height falls will continue to increase as the 
stronger vort maxima moves overhead, along with surface pressure 
falls. While convergence will be increasing, current guidance 
focuses the moisture transport from the Ozarks into eastern 
Missouri, while in central and western Missouri, the air remains 
drier that may be the result of the dry conveyor belt wrapping 
around the low. Because of this, rain chances continue to remain 
east of Hwy. 63 Wednesday into Thursday, and mostly just outside of 
the forecast area. Current ensemble probabilities maintain less than 
10 percent chance for measurable rain for our eastern zones, with 
higher probabilities into eastern Missouri. There may be enough lift 
to generate some light sprinkle activity, but overall forcing and 
moisture are aligning better further east.  

The trailing cold front is progged to move through late Wednesday 
Night and early Thursday morning, which will push temperatures back 
down into the lower and mid 50s heading into the weekend. A strong 
500mb ridge is expected to amplify over the western CONUS during the 
weekend, reinforcing northwesterly flow into the Central CONUS which 
will keep temperatures generally in the 50s. This may start 
deamplify late Saturday, bringing mid-level height falls back across 
the forecast area on Sunday, where light rain activity may be 
possible.
