.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 149 PM CDT Sun Oct 25 2026

Upper-level ridging will shift eastward and become less amplified 
Monday into Tuesday. This will will allow a short wave trough to 
move out of Mexico and southwestern Texas and into the Middle to 
Lower Missouri valley by Tuesday evening. Forcing associated with 
this shortwave may be enough to wring out the marginal moisture 
available to the system. As a result, isolated to scattered showers, 
and potentially a thunderstorm or two, look possible during the day 
across eastern Kansas and western Missouri. The potential for 
measurable rainfall is about 20-30%. The potential for any storms is 
still very low and generally less than 10%. So, while there is 
some potential, it's more than likely just going to be showers 
moving through, if there is any precipitation. 

Otherwise, above normal temperatures will continue. For the Kansas 
City area, normal high temperatures range from 63 on Monday, to 60 
by Sunday. Forecast highs on Monday are in the upper 70s, about 15 
degrees above normal for this time of year. Temperatures drop off 
slightly on Tuesday with the shortwave and associated cloud cover 
and precipitation chances moving into the area through the day.
For the middle part of the work week, while the ridging remains
mostly situated southeast of the region, persistent southerly 
flow around the ridging/ surface high to our southeast, will 
maintain warm advection across the Southern and Central Plains. 
So it looks likely we'll see highs rebound back into the upper 
70s to potentially lower 80s, about 15-20 degrees above normal. 
Late in the week, precipitation chances start to increase, with 
scattered to widespread rainfall by Saturday. Thursday, a front 
sags southward into the area, pushed southward by a shortwave 
tracking across the Northern Plains and Upper Midwest. That 
front interacts with increasing moisture from the persistent 
southerly flow to give us our increasing precipitation chances. 
For the weekend, a series of waves moves through the area, with 
front still in the vicinity. With broad upper forcing, the 
boundary in the area, and moisture available, we should see 
scattered to widespread showers Friday night into Saturday. 
Right now, it looks like a 40-60% chance for rain from Saturday
afternoon into Saturday evening, with decreasing chances after 
midnight. So umbrellas may be needed for anyone outside during 
that time frame. Still, though it looks like we'll see scattered
to widespread precipitation, rain amounts look relatively low 
through the weekend. For the 48 hour period ending at 6 am 
Sunday morning, there is roughly a 1 in 3 (30-35%) chance for 
rain amounts exceeding 1 inch and just over a coin flip (50-60%)
for exceeding half an inch.
