How flags are set (thresholds)

FIRST WARN WEATHER DAY (potentially dangerous; supersedes First Warn): SPC Day 1–3 categorical Slight (2/5)+ over KC · SPC Day 4–8 ≥15% area over KC · snow ≥1" at KCI (NWS gridpoint snowfall, model-median best-fit snow from the winter section, or winter event guidance median) · freezing-rain ice ≥0.01" (NWS gridpoint or model median) · wind chill ≤-10°F or heat index ≥105°F (NWS gridpoint) · any NWS warning (except river/creek Flood Warnings, shown as notes only, and Freeze Warnings, which are First Warn only for plant impacts), flood/flash-flood/severe/winter-storm watch, winter-weather/cold/heat advisory for a DMA point.

FIRST WARN (disruptive, not necessarily dangerous): daytime (6 AM–8 PM CT) NWS gridpoint hourly PoP >50% (beyond the NWS grid or if NWS fails: >50% of models with ≥0.01" daytime precip; commute windows 6–9 AM / 4–7 PM noted) · dense fog: NWS gridpoint visibility ≤0.25 mi or Dense Fog Advisory (model visibility is not in our model feed) · gusts ≥40 mph (NWS gridpoint or model-median peak) · high or daytime feels-like drops ≥15°F day to day (day 1 vs yesterday’s observed at KCI; feels-like = 7 AM–7 PM average) · light wintry mix without accumulation (NWS weather likely+, or ≥half the models) · Wind/Frost/Dense Smoke advisories · Freeze Warning (sensitive plants; not a Weather Day).

KCI point (39.30, −94.71); SPC checked at downtown KC. Models: all non-experimental models on this page (the same flags on both sites). Hourly checks only use hours from now on. A failed source shows as “unavailable” in the tooltip. Thresholds live in one block: CFG in firstwarn.py.

eastern CWA. Light snow is expected to move southwest through the

freezing and as such, snow has struggled to stick to anything but 
elevated surfaces. With warm surface temperatures, ground 
temperatures, and light snowfall rates, it my be difficult for snow 
to accumulate on sidewalks, and roadways unless rates can overcome 
warm ground and air temps. However snow will accumulate on grassy 
surfaces as well as elevated surface like bridges and overpasses.

morning/early afternoon, 1-2 inches are forecast for areas east of 
Highway 65. From Highway 65 back to western Missouri only a dusting

the KC Metro. As the system exits this afternoon highs are expected 
to rise into the upper 30s to lower 40s fostering additional 
melting. A quick moving weak surface ridge of high pressure is 
expected to move through the area tonight aiding in lows dropping 
back into the low to mid 20s. This surface ridge will slide south 
and east of the area tomorrow with WAA getting underway as the upper 
level flow become quasi-zonal. This will allow highs to mid 40s to 
mid 50s. Tomorrow night a upper level shortwave trough with dig 
through the northern Plains into the Upper Midwest. This will force 
more of a Pacific front through the CWA and downslope westerly in 
the wake of the system will aid in highs rising into the 50s on 
Saturday. 

Saturday night a upper level trough will move from the eastern 
Rockies into the Plains forcing a cold front into the area and 
bringing showers (70-90%) to the CWA. Showers will exit the area by 
Sunday afternoon however, CAA behind the front will drop highs into 
the 40s to near 50. Monday, upper level flow becomes zonal across 
the region with late day WAA getting underway which will push highs 
into the mid 40s to mid 50s. Zonal upper level flow will continue on 
Tuesday with continued WAA raising highs into the 50s. Tuesday night 
into Wednesday, a upper level trough will dig into the Four Corners 
region allow lee cyclogenesis to deepen. This will tighten the 
pressure gradient across the area with strong WAA expected. That 
will push highs up into the mid 50s to mid 60s on Wednesday.
