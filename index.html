How flags are set (thresholds)

FIRST WARN WEATHER DAY (potentially dangerous; supersedes First Warn): SPC Day 1–3 categorical Slight (2/5)+ over KC · SPC Day 4–8 ≥15% area over KC · snow ≥1" at KCI (NWS gridpoint snowfall, model-median best-fit snow from the winter section, or winter event guidance median) · freezing-rain ice ≥0.01" (NWS gridpoint or model median) · wind chill ≤-10°F or heat index ≥105°F (NWS gridpoint) · any NWS warning (except river/creek Flood Warnings, shown as notes only, and Freeze Warnings, which are First Warn only for plant impacts), flood/flash-flood/severe/winter-storm watch, winter-weather/cold/heat advisory for a DMA point.

FIRST WARN (disruptive, not necessarily dangerous): daytime (6 AM–8 PM CT) NWS gridpoint hourly PoP >50% (beyond the NWS grid or if NWS fails: >50% of models with ≥0.01" daytime precip; commute windows 6–9 AM / 4–7 PM noted) · dense fog: NWS gridpoint visibility ≤0.25 mi or Dense Fog Advisory (model visibility is not in our model feed) · gusts ≥40 mph (NWS gridpoint or model-median peak) · high drops ≥20°F day to day · light wintry mix without accumulation (NWS weather likely+, or ≥half the models) · Wind/Frost/Dense Smoke advisories · Freeze Warning (sensitive plants; not a Weather Day).

.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 302 AM CST Wed Nov 11 2026

Today will be the last day of unseasonably warm temperatures
(10-15 degrees above average) as influence from the 500mb jet 
stream brings us into a pattern change. The jet is continuing to
dig southward and as a result, an associated low pressure 
center (currently centered over west-central Iowa as of 09z) 
will continue to progress southeastward into central Illinois by
late morning. As a result, a cold front will pass through 
Missouri during the morning hours, which will result in a wind 
shift from southwest to northwest. Temperatures will still be 
able to climb into the 60s as the dynamics will allow for 
enhanced mixing. With the low pressure system to our east, the 
area will see a pressure gradient, which will result in wind 
speeds increasing to 15-20kts, with gusts approaching or 
slightly exceeding 30kts. Winds die down in the evening as the 
low continues to progress to the east. During the Wednesday 
night into Thursday morning time frame, a secondary cold front 
the north will progress into our region. This will allow for 
cold air advection to increase overnight and throughout the day 
Thursday. Because of this cold surge, highs on Thursday are only
expected to get up into the mid 40s to low 50s.

By the time we get to Thursday night into Friday morning, skies
will be mostly clear, with higher concentrations of clouds in 
eastern Missouri. This will allow for much of the state to see 
temperatures below freezing, getting down to the mid 20s. Since 
the climatological date for our first hard-freeze has passed, 
any type of issuance to reflect a frost/freeze headline will not
be issued. Regardless, any sensitive plants will need to be 
protected. The trough will continue to dig, and temperatures 
will remain in the 40s as the jet will keep milder temperatures 
to our south. Temperatures below freezing are expected overnight
Friday into Saturday Morning.

The weather will remain quiet during the day on Saturday as we 
continue to see influence from our airmass from the north with 
response from the jet stream and surface high pressure north of 
Lake Superior. By the middle of the weekend, the pattern becomes
much more zonal, but a shortwave may bring the return of rain 
on Sunday into Monday. Highest chances remain to our south and 
west, but exact totals and spatial coverage for our area will 
become more clear as we get closer to the weekend.
