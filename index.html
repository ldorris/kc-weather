How flags are set (thresholds)

FIRST WARN WEATHER DAY (potentially dangerous; supersedes First Warn): SPC Day 1–3 categorical Slight (2/5)+ over KC · SPC Day 4–8 ≥15% area over KC · snow ≥1" at KCI (NWS gridpoint snowfall, model-median best-fit snow from the winter section, or winter event guidance median) · freezing-rain ice ≥0.01" (NWS gridpoint or model median) · wind chill ≤-10°F or heat index ≥105°F (NWS gridpoint) · any NWS warning, flood/flash-flood/severe/winter-storm watch, winter-weather/cold/heat advisory for a DMA point.

FIRST WARN (disruptive, not necessarily dangerous): daytime (6 AM–8 PM CT) NWS gridpoint hourly PoP >50% (beyond the NWS grid or if NWS fails: >50% of models with ≥0.01" daytime precip; commute windows 6–9 AM / 4–7 PM noted) · dense fog: NWS gridpoint visibility ≤0.25 mi or Dense Fog Advisory (model visibility is not in our model feed) · gusts ≥40 mph (NWS gridpoint or model-median peak) · high drops ≥20°F day to day · light wintry mix without accumulation (NWS weather likely+, or ≥half the models) · Wind/Frost/Dense Smoke advisories.

Issued at 232 PM CDT Fri Oct 30 2026

Comparison of 00Z and 12Z upper-air observations detail a deepening 
upper-level trough over the Pacific Northwest and continued broad 
ridging/near-zonal flow more centrally located over the CONUS. 
Mid/low-level flow has become increasingly southerly/southwesterly 
overnight and into the morning, advecting moisture into the region. 
A mid-level shortwave mapped over southwest Texas is set to propel 
northeastward into the the Central Plains later today. A surface 
boundary that moved through the area yesterday now rests over 
central Missouri but is expected to lift north today as a warm 
front, bringing with it renewed rain and storm chances. Over the 
course of the afternoon, the expectation is for elevated instability 
to rise to around 500 J/kg. Based on forecast soundings, however, 
this layer of instability is rather shallow. There may be a few 
cells capable of producing some lightning, but current thinking is 
that most will remain as light rain showers. Rain chances will 
increase to about 40-50% south of the Missouri River by 6 PM or so 
before expanding further north overnight. Confidence in rain is 
greatest primarily in areas west of I-35 where hourly PoPs approach 
70-80% just around midnight. WPC has maintained a Marginal Risk 
(Level 1 of 4) for excessive rainfall for portions of northern 
Missouri and northeastern Kansas. PWATs range from about 1.25" 
across northern Missouri to 1.5" further south as well as closer to 
the MO/KS border and Kansas City metro. 15-mile neighborhood 
probabilities for 24-hour rainfall accumulations of at least 0.25" 
by 1 PM Saturday are lowest (~15%) in central Missouri and increase 
further to the northwest. The highest probability corridor sits 
roughly between I-29 and I-35 in Missouri (including the Kansas City 
metro) where neighborhood probabilities begin to exceed 80% for at 
least 0.25" of rain. Model spread is also higher in these areas 
(generally a 0.25-0.5" difference between the 25th and 75th 
percentiles). All that said...generally expecting around 0.25" to 
0.5" of rain west of I-35 through early Saturday afternoon. 
Elsewhere, accumulations will likely fall below 0.25". Locally 
higher amounts will be possible, but these values should provide a 
broad overview of expectations. 

Isolated to scattered showers will continue Saturday afternoon and 
potentially into the evening. The mid-level trough is anticipated to
