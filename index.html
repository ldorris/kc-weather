.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 248 PM CDT Sat Oct 24 2026

Another dry and warm day across the region. Southerly warm air 
advection pushes highs toward 80 degrees for most of the area which 
is well above seasonal expectations of 64F. Precipitation chances 
for early week remain minimal as rain is more likely to stay north 
and west. More widespread precipitation chances return late week; 
however, uncertainty remains fairly high with the spatial extent of 
synoptic features and associated rainfall.

Beyond the beautiful weather conditions today, water vapor satellite 
imagery shows a complex interconnected web of weather systems that 
will influence our forecast over the next week. A trough across the 
SW CONUS has cut off from the overall flow as anticipated, a ridge 
builds across the central CONUS, another low pressure system 
migrates through interior Canada, and another trough across the SE 
CONUS compliments the western CONUS trough. The satellite picture 
yesterday morning did not present such a clear picture of the 
emerging weather systems; however, most all guidance did pick up on 
the evolution of the SW CONUS cut off low which is one of the 
critical influencer in this week's weather. This does increase 
confidence and reduce uncertainties, but does not eliminate them.

All the previous mentioned systems were at the 500mb level, at the 
low to mid levels a large high sits over the Ohio Valley as analyzed 
on the SPC 850mb analysis. This high has been gradually expanding 
westward pushing stable descending air into the region acting as one 
of the primary drivers our pleasant conditions over the past 
several days. Warm air advection from southerly oriented low to mid 
level flow has been pushing our high temperatures well above 
seasonal expectations. One other characteristic of this air mass has 
been how dry it is with dew points in the 40s. Most sounding 
analysis has lifted condensation levels around 7000ft and ceilings 
or where the air is much closer to saturation around 20,000ft making 
short term PoPs pretty much zero.

One of the most significant components to our weather picture is not 
even in the CONUS at this moment. A pair of 500mb lows across Canada 
have a midlevel high sandwiched between there respective positions. 
This high is expected to eject out toward the central CONUS this 
push of cooler Canadian air is expected to promote the development 
of a synoptic scale front across the interior of the CONUS, a small 
semblance can be seen with clouds across the Dakotas. Ejecting 
shortwaves from the SW CONUS low have been sparking off convection 
across the boundary for several days. As the midlevel high moves 
southeastward, this synoptic front pushes towards our region. 
Simultaneously, the low across interior Canada migrates eastward and 
the SW CONUS low starts getting absorbed into the overall flow 
across the continent. This stretches the frontal boundary almost 
wrapping it to the west and north of our region. This also slows the 
progression of the lower level high which weakens the thermal and 
dynamic gradients of the front. All of this combined with the dry 
air in place across our area results in relatively low precipitation 
chances (10-20%) Tuesday through Thursday, mainly during the morning 
hours. As the front wraps around eastern Nebraska into Iowa much of 
the precipitation potential looks limited to far NW MO. Furthermore, 
referring to the sounding analysis mentioned earlier, any 
precipitation development will have to overcome a fairly deep 5000ft 
dry layer before reaching the surface. The lack of significant 
temperature gradients, moisture boundaries, and instability, do not 
promote much confidence in precipitation potential. However, those 
are not enough confidence nudgers to say there will not be rain, it 
just has many challenges.

Once the front settled into position it looks to sit there through 
the end of next week. Aloft, flow becomes more progressive and the 
general divide between the cooler north jet and the subtropical jet 
sinks southward. This lowers high temperatures slightly on Tuesday 
to the low 70s while shortwave perturbations build up across 
Rockies. As the upper level pattern shifts this shortwave pass by 
the region being deflected by the still residing lower level high 
across the SE CONUS. This deflects systems northward, but does keep 
low PoP chances across NW MO Wednesday as they pass by the area. 
This also works to bump our high temperatures Wednesday back to the 
mid-70s.

Getting to late week ensemble guidance slowly drifts the synoptic 
frontal boundary closer to the area. The upper level pattern remains 
fairly progressive with more frequent upper level waves moving 
across the country. Ensemble guidance suggests this will create a 
pattern of frequent midlevel lee troughs progressing along the 
boundary using the kinematic and thermal gradients to initiate 
showers. This pattern looks to keep rain chances in the forecast 
through the weekend. Many uncertainties remain with this solution as 
it is heavily dependent on the locations of the front and pressure 
perturbations. With this many subtle deviations possible even 
deterministic models tend to broad brush precipitation chances. 
Uncertainty is likely to narrow as the pattern more firmly 
establishes itself Monday and Tuesday.
