.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 158 AM CST Sun Nov 1 2026

Precipitation chances will decrease through the morning hours today 
as shortwave ridging builds eastward into the area. However, 
moisture will be trapped in the lower portions of the atmosphere so 
we'll continue to see cloud cover. This should keep highs in the 60s 
across the area.

Focus shifts to Monday afternoon through the overnight hours as a 
strong shortwave trough moves east into the central US. A surface 
low will deepen across Nebraska with a front trailing southward 
through Kansas. Very strong southerly low-level flow will lead to 
anomalously high precipitable water values, supporting potential for 
heavy rainfall. In the upper-levels, a leading jet max may help
initiate storms in the warm sector. But's a trailing, stronger 
jet max will move across the area later in the day and into the 
overnight hours. And that seems to be the more likely time frame
for severe storms across eastern Kansas into western Missouri. 
Storms seem likely to initiate along the cold front in central 
Kansas and Oklahoma during the afternoon. These may be 
supercells initially but these should develop into a line as 
they move east late in the afternoon and evening, with damaging 
winds being the most likely hazard. Favorable 0-3KM shear of 
30-40kts may also support embedded circulations within the line 
of storms. With 0-.5km helicity values pushing 200 m2/s2 can't 
rule out a tornado or two over eastern Kansas and adjacent areas
of Missouri as the line of storms moves east during the evening
and overnight hours. The Slight Risk area was expanded 
northeastward on the latest Day 2 Convective Outlook, with much 
of western Missouri and eastern Kansas in a Marginal Risk. 

Unsettled weather will linger into Tuesday, though how unsettled it 
may be is a little uncertain. The deterministic NAM is considerably 
slower with the upper wave and and actually has some potential for 
severe weather Tuesday morning. The GFS is further east with 
potential storms east of our forecast area. With the upper low 
moving over Kansas into Missouri through the day, we should still 
see some potential for precipitation through the day Tuesday. 

Temperatures through the remainder of the forecast look cooler, 
though still slightly above normal for this time of year, with highs 
generally in the low to mid 60s.
