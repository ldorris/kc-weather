.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 227 PM CDT Thu Oct 29 2026

What has been generally zonal flow aloft will become increasingly 
meridional over the coming days, leading to a more active period for 
weather. A cold front has slipped southward throughout the morning. 
Latest surface observations indicate this boundary now sits near the 
Missouri River with temperatures roughly 3-5 degF cooler on the 
north side of front. As for high temperatures today, we could see 
about a 10 degF temperature difference between our northern and 
southern tiers of counties. The front is expected to continue 
sinking south throughout the afternoon and evening, becoming more 
diffuse in the process. Isolated shower or storm development south 
of the boundary this afternoon remains possible under WAA 
conditions, but it seems such development would be quite the uphill 
battle given poor shear and lackluster lapse rates. The arrival of 
the cold front could inspire a brief shower or storm. Even so, I 
would not expect anything remotely robust given the lack of upper-
level support. Shower and storm activity appears to be more likely 
(40-70%) across northeastern Kansas and northern Missouri beginning 
later this evening and then continuing through the early portions of 
the day on Friday. Continued low-level WAA will promote a region of 
elevated instability (as much as 800 J/kg) in these areas by late 
evening. Lift looks to be provided by an approaching shortwave with 
several members of the guidance resolving a localized vortmax north 
of the Missouri River around 06Z; roughly around this time is when 
CAMs also begin to resolve scattered showers and storms. CAMs also 
present a backbuilding scenario across far northeast Kansas into 
northwest Missouri...with PWATs between 1.25-1.5", isolated flooding 
may occur where showers/storms continuously move over the same area.

Scattered showers and storms will continue across portions of 
northern Kansas and Missouri for much of the day on Friday. Further 
south, the cold front currently crawling through our CWA will lift 
back north as a warm front in the afternoon/evening. This with an 
approaching mid-level trough will reinvigorate rain chances for much 
of the region Friday PM into Saturday AM (hourly PoPs about 40-70%). 
Low-end rain chances will continue for the remainder of the day as 
the mid-level trough continues its journey through the 
Plains/Midwest. Generally along and west of the I-35 and I-49 
corridors, these chances are closer to 20-30% given a low-level dry 
layer and little upper-level support from the vacating mid-level 
trough. Further into central Missouri, these rain chances are 
slightly higher at 30-50% where the mid-level trough may still 
retain some influence.

Sunday continues to look dry with temperatures in the upper 60s/low 
70s. A deepening upper trough over the western CONUS will slide to 
eastward over the weekend with its accompanying surface cyclone 
moving into the Great Plains on Monday. This system will result in a 
more active weather pattern than what we have become accustomed to 
in recent days and even some weeks. Indeed, the first half of the 
week looks rather soggy (Monday and Tuesday especially). 
Unsurprisingly, the entire CWA as well as a large chunk of the 
Central Plains are included in a Day 5 Marginal Risk (Level 1 of 4) 
for excessive rainfall on Monday, Nov. 2. We will need to monitor 
potential instability corridors ahead of the  surface 
low's attendant cold front as these areas would have an inherently 
higher risk for flooding due to convectively-driven high rainfall 
rates.
