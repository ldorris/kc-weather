How flags are set (thresholds)

FIRST WARN WEATHER DAY (potentially dangerous; supersedes First Warn): SPC Day 1–3 categorical Slight (2/5)+ over KC · SPC Day 4–8 ≥15% area over KC · snow ≥1" at KCI (NWS gridpoint snowfall, model-median best-fit snow from the winter section, or winter event guidance median) · freezing-rain ice ≥0.01" (NWS gridpoint or model median) · wind chill ≤-10°F or heat index ≥105°F (NWS gridpoint) · any NWS warning (except river/creek Flood Warnings, shown as notes only, and Freeze Warnings, which are First Warn only for plant impacts), flood/flash-flood/severe/winter-storm watch, winter-weather/cold/heat advisory for a DMA point.

FIRST WARN (disruptive, not necessarily dangerous): daytime (6 AM–8 PM CT) NWS gridpoint hourly PoP >50% (beyond the NWS grid or if NWS fails: >50% of models with ≥0.01" daytime precip; commute windows 6–9 AM / 4–7 PM noted) · dense fog: NWS gridpoint visibility ≤0.25 mi or Dense Fog Advisory (model visibility is not in our model feed) · gusts ≥40 mph (NWS gridpoint or model-median peak) · high drops ≥20°F day to day · light wintry mix without accumulation (NWS weather likely+, or ≥half the models) · Wind/Frost/Dense Smoke advisories · Freeze Warning (sensitive plants; not a Weather Day).

Dry and unseasonably warm conditions (5-10 degrees above normal) 
usher in the week as surface high pressure sprawls across the lower 
Mississippi River Valley with northwesterly flow aloft. Light 
diurnally-driven winds will go calm overnight, and clear skies will 
allow for efficient radiational cooling. Consequently, widespread 
fog (with some localized dense fog) is likely in west-central MO and 
eastern KS, with REFS probabilities for visibility below 2 miles 
between 50-70% by 12Z Monday morning. A 500mb low begins to trek 
into Texas on Monday while 500mb ridging builds across the SE CONUS, 
which will strengthen our southerly flow and WAA. Highs Monday 
and Tuesday will range from the mid 60s in northern MO to the 
low 70s south of the Missouri River. 

The streak of non-November like temperatures comes to an end 
Wednesday and Thursday. An upper-level trough will deepen across the 
upper-Midwest and Great-Lakes region Wednesday while a secondary 
wave translates the southern Rockies. An attendant surface-low 
develops in the SD/IA region Wednesday, sliding into eastern 
MO/western IL during the day. Given the timing and position of both 
waves in relation to one another and to the surface low, the 
strongest forcing and lift will stay east of our CWA, limiting rain 
potential Wednesday and Thursday. The highest POPs (~10%) are 
confined along and east of the US63 corridor in MO. Accumulations 
will be light - the LREF 90th percentile for accumulated 
precipitation is generally around .1" (mean is around .05") in 
central MO. 

Strong NW flow in the wake of the midweek system will bring highs 
down into the upper 40s to low 50s to finish out the work week. 
Patchy frost will also be possible Saturday morning, with light 
winds and lows ranging from the upper 20s in northern MO to low 30s 
in central MO. A familiar pattern takes shape next weekend, with 
upper-level low pressure across the Desert SW and surface high 
pressure in place over the Mississippi River Valley. Southerly flow 
will increase as a result, bringing temperatures back into the 60s 
by Sunday.
