How flags are set (thresholds)

FIRST WARN WEATHER DAY (potentially dangerous; supersedes First Warn): SPC Day 1–3 categorical Slight (2/5)+ over KC · SPC Day 4–8 ≥15% area over KC · snow ≥1" at KCI (NWS gridpoint snowfall, model-median best-fit snow from the winter section, or winter event guidance median) · freezing-rain ice ≥0.01" (NWS gridpoint or model median) · wind chill ≤-10°F or heat index ≥105°F (NWS gridpoint) · any NWS warning, flood/flash-flood/severe/winter-storm watch, winter-weather/cold/heat advisory for a DMA point.

FIRST WARN (disruptive, not necessarily dangerous): daytime (6 AM–8 PM CT) NWS gridpoint hourly PoP >50% (beyond the NWS grid or if NWS fails: >50% of models with ≥0.01" daytime precip; commute windows 6–9 AM / 4–7 PM noted) · dense fog: NWS gridpoint visibility ≤0.25 mi or Dense Fog Advisory (model visibility is not in our model feed) · gusts ≥40 mph (NWS gridpoint or model-median peak) · high drops ≥20°F day to day · light wintry mix without accumulation (NWS weather likely+, or ≥half the models) · Wind/Frost/Dense Smoke advisories.

.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 201 PM CST Sun Nov 1 2026

Mid to upper-level synoptic pattern features an amplifying ridge 
axis that is currently over the Central Plains and moving eastward, 
being driven by a deep trough digging toward the Four Corners Region 
this afternoon. For the lower Missouri River Valley for the 
remainder of Sunday, a few breaks in cloud cover may be possible 
with mid-level AVA generating some subsidence. However, flow around 
700mb continues to bring in plenty of moisture, which will limit the 
ability to completely clear the overcast and misty conditions across 
the forecast area. The ridge axis races east of the Mississippi 
River Valley overnight, as the trough acquires a negative tilt and 
lifts out of the Four Corners Region toward the Rockies. Mid-level 
height falls will extend eastward into the lower Missouri River 
Valley. The strong dCVA into the Front Range and High Plains will 
foster lee cyclogenesis for most of Monday, tracking a strong 
cyclone into the Central Plains. Low-level flow from central Kansas 
into the Mississippi River Valley will turn southerly, and increase 
theta-e transport, with a notable jump in dewpoint temperatures by 
Monday afternoon. For our forecast area, this will start to push a 
warm front northward, bringing with it increasing isentropic upglide 
and perhaps some enhanced mid-level lift from any vorticity lobes 
that eject eastward into the central CONUS. For eastern Kansas to 
Central Missouri, this will likely keep dense cloud cover, unless 
something Sunday evening helps to drastically clear it. The better 
clearing within this synoptic pattern will be in south-central 
Kansas and regions southward where a more notable EML is noted. 
Scattered showers are likely along the nose of stronger isentropic 
upglide Monday morning through early afternoon. At this time, 
instability does not look overly impressive, with HREF mean MUCAPE 
values struggling to reach 1000 J/kg. However, depending on how 
faster the 500mb trough axis moves, mid-level cooling may help to 
steepen mid-level lapse rates by the early afternoon, which may 
produce some thunderstorm activity. An isolated severe storm prior 
to 20z is possible if there is any kind of mesoscale clearing that 
can help locally destabilize the boundary layer, as deep layer shear 
with strong mid-level let jet will be present (0-6km bulk shear 
values above 40kts possible over the warm sector). But, overall 
forcing will be minimal and the lack of insolation may inhibit deep 
convection. 

Late afternoon, the rate of mid-level height falls increases, along 
with convergence along the deepening cyclone and its trailing cold 
front. Continued theta-e advection will provide more moisture into 
the area, and will help bring in a somewhat more unstable airmass 
that could see SBCAPE attempt to near 1000 J/kg. The instability is 
the greatest source of uncertainty with how the severe threat will 
play out for eastern Kansas and western Missouri late afternoon 
Monday and through the evening. If there is any kind of localized 
subsidence prior to sunset that destabilizes the boundary layer with 
dewpoints in the mid 60s, mid-level cooling that steepens lapse
