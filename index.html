How flags are set (thresholds)

FIRST WARN WEATHER DAY (potentially dangerous; supersedes First Warn): SPC Day 1–3 categorical Slight (2/5)+ over KC · SPC Day 4–8 ≥15% area over KC · snow ≥1" at KCI (NWS gridpoint snowfall, model-median best-fit snow from the winter section, or winter event guidance median) · freezing-rain ice ≥0.01" (NWS gridpoint or model median) · wind chill ≤-10°F or heat index ≥105°F (NWS gridpoint) · any NWS warning (except river/creek Flood Warnings, shown as notes only, and Freeze Warnings, which are First Warn only for plant impacts), flood/flash-flood/severe/winter-storm watch, winter-weather/cold/heat advisory for a DMA point.

FIRST WARN (disruptive, not necessarily dangerous): daytime (6 AM–8 PM CT) NWS gridpoint hourly PoP >50% (beyond the NWS grid or if NWS fails: >50% of models with ≥0.01" daytime precip; commute windows 6–9 AM / 4–7 PM noted) · dense fog: NWS gridpoint visibility ≤0.25 mi or Dense Fog Advisory (model visibility is not in our model feed) · gusts ≥40 mph (NWS gridpoint or model-median peak) · high or daytime feels-like drops ≥15°F day to day (day 1 vs yesterday’s observed at KCI; feels-like = 7 AM–7 PM average) · light wintry mix without accumulation (NWS weather likely+, or ≥half the models) · Wind/Frost/Dense Smoke advisories · Freeze Warning (sensitive plants; not a Weather Day).

KCI point (39.30, −94.71); SPC checked at downtown KC. Models: all non-experimental models on this page (the same flags on both sites). Hourly checks only use hours from now on. A failed source shows as “unavailable” in the tooltip. Thresholds live in one block: CFG in firstwarn.py.

yesterday, though, marginally improved low-level moisture transport 
with the progression of surface high pressure further east will help 
boost RH values a bit, keeping conditions below critical fire 
weather thresholds during the daytime.

southeastward from the northern Plains. Ongoing south/southwesterly 
flow in the low-levels will maintain daytime temperatures in the

CAPE. The high shear environment, coupled with steepening lapse 
rates out ahead of the front and robust synoptic lift, increases 
confidence of organized convective activity occurring Saturday 
evening with the passage of the front. However, confidence on severe 
likelihood continues to remain low, with marginal overall 
instability bringing the intensity of that convection into question.

daytime temperatures fall back to the upper 40s/low 50s as northerly

Plains. Ongoing advection of cool, dry air from the north/northeast 
will keep the beginning of next week quiet on the precipitation

overnight lows drop below freezing. By the end of the period, global 
guidance diverges in its resolution of the overall synoptic pattern, 
decreasing overall confidence in any resolution of precipitation for 
the long term forecast. Future forecasts will have to monitor long 
term trends to find greater consensus in the synoptic pattern. As of 
this forecast cycle, though, the emerging patterns of global 
guidance keep things cool and dry for eastern KS and
northwestern MO.
