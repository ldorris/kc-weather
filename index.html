How flags are set (thresholds)

FIRST WARN WEATHER DAY (potentially dangerous; supersedes First Warn): SPC Day 1–3 categorical Slight (2/5)+ over KC · SPC Day 4–8 ≥15% area over KC · snow ≥1" at KCI (NWS gridpoint snowfall, model-median best-fit snow from the winter section, or winter event guidance median) · freezing-rain ice ≥0.01" (NWS gridpoint or model median) · wind chill ≤-10°F or heat index ≥105°F (NWS gridpoint) · any NWS warning, flood/flash-flood/severe/winter-storm watch, winter-weather/cold/heat advisory for a DMA point.

FIRST WARN (disruptive, not necessarily dangerous): daytime (6 AM–8 PM CT) NWS gridpoint hourly PoP >50% (beyond the NWS grid or if NWS fails: >50% of models with ≥0.01" daytime precip; commute windows 6–9 AM / 4–7 PM noted) · dense fog: NWS gridpoint visibility ≤0.25 mi or Dense Fog Advisory (model visibility is not in our model feed) · gusts ≥40 mph (NWS gridpoint or model-median peak) · high drops ≥20°F day to day · light wintry mix without accumulation (NWS weather likely+, or ≥half the models) · Wind/Frost/Dense Smoke advisories.

.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 125 AM CST Tue Nov 3 2026

The upper system responsible for the storms and the severe weather 
risk on Monday (that fortunately didn't materialize in our forecast 
area) will be slow to shift to the east today as it becomes more 
negatively tilted over northeastern Kansas. As the upper low pivots 
this morning, low-level flow will wrap into the surface low with 
locally backed surface winds over northeastern Kansas and 
northwestern Missouri. Forecast soundings show modest, albeit 
somewhat shallow CAPE with little or no inhibition by late this 
morning/early this afternoon. This may result in low-topped 
convection and given the very strong shear may lead to low-topped 
supercells late this morning to early this afternoon. This risk 
appears greatest just northeast of the apparent triple point from 
far northeastern Kansas into far northwestern Missouri. There are 
some caveats with this. 1) This system is almost vertically stacked, 
which is less favorable than having an offset surface low and triple 
point of about 100-200 miles. 2) shear is extremely strong and 
mostly unidirectional, which may just rip apart updrafts. 

For the remainder of the week and into the weekend, cooler but 
more seasonable conditions settle into the area. Normal highs 
for the area now in the upper 50s and our forecast shows highs 
within a few degrees of our normals through the weekend. The 
rest of forecast looks dry with no notable systems moving 
through the area through Saturday. The GFS continues to show a 
robust wave diving into the eastern half of the CONUS. This is 
considerably further east than yesterday and by early next week,
upper ridging begins to build into the area, leading to 
temperatures trending above normal.

before fully subsiding. Uncertain extent of MVFR cloud cover 
overnight before cold front approaches with another round of
