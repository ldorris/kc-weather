How flags are set (thresholds)

FIRST WARN WEATHER DAY (potentially dangerous; supersedes First Warn): SPC Day 1–3 categorical Slight (2/5)+ over KC · SPC Day 4–8 ≥15% area over KC · snow ≥1" at KCI (NWS gridpoint snowfall, model-median best-fit snow from the winter section, or winter event guidance median) · freezing-rain ice ≥0.01" (NWS gridpoint or model median) · wind chill ≤-10°F or heat index ≥105°F (NWS gridpoint) · any NWS warning, flood/flash-flood/severe/winter-storm watch, winter-weather/cold/heat advisory for a DMA point.

FIRST WARN (disruptive, not necessarily dangerous): daytime (6 AM–8 PM CT) NWS gridpoint hourly PoP >50% (beyond the NWS grid or if NWS fails: >50% of models with ≥0.01" daytime precip; commute windows 6–9 AM / 4–7 PM noted) · dense fog: NWS gridpoint visibility ≤0.25 mi or Dense Fog Advisory (model visibility is not in our model feed) · gusts ≥40 mph (NWS gridpoint or model-median peak) · high drops ≥20°F day to day · light wintry mix without accumulation (NWS weather likely+, or ≥half the models) · Wind/Frost/Dense Smoke advisories.

KCI point (39.30, −94.71); SPC checked at downtown KC. Models: all non-experimental models on this page (the same flags on both sites). Hourly checks only use hours from now on. A failed source shows as “unavailable” in the tooltip. Thresholds live in one block: CFG in firstwarn.py.

.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 158 AM CST Mon Nov 2 2026

This is a very complex forecast over the next 24 to 36 hours as
a deepening trough moves out of the Intermountain West into the
Plains. Water vapor imagery early this morning shows this 
system centered roughly over Utah and somewhat disorganized 
still. A subtropical jet is surging across the Southwest into 
eastern New Mexico and the northwest Texas Panhandle, with 
convection already noted in that region. The polar jet is 
rounding the base of the trough currently and remains discrete 
from the subtropical jet initially. Broad and deep ascent will 
increase later this morning and this afternoon, associated with 
the subtropical jet moving into the area. This forcing may get 
an initial round of showers and storms to develop in the 
noon-3pm time frame. CAPE is going to be limited for this entire
event and that will be the running theme throughout the next 
24-36 hours. But if sufficient surface based CAPE can develop 
during the day, and it isn't constantly eroded from the strong 
forcing overspreading the area, these initial cells could become
severe. This seems unlikely though. What seems more likely is 
storm development along the front through central-eastern Kansas
during the afternoon, where instability is maximized just ahead
of the boundary. These storms will likely quickly become linear
or semi-discrete clusters as the whole area moves east from the
late afternoon into the evening hours. As this is occurring, 
the low- level jet strengthens, enhancing the curvature of the 
low-level hodograph and lengthening it overall across eastern 
Kansas and into far western Missouri, increasing the potential 
for embedded tornados within the line. Ensemble mean MUCAPE
peaks around 500 J/kg in far eastern Kansas and western 
Missouri. Again, very limited but may be sufficient given the
shear. With this line of storms moving into our western zones 
as early as 02-04Z and continuing east through 06-08Z, we may be
dealing with a damaging wind and QLCS tornado risk well into 
the overnight hours before the system loses steam in the pre- 
dawn hours. 

Tuesday continues to look unsettled and may pose a sneaky severe 
risk with some instability ahead of the slow moving front late 
in the morning to early afternoon. The NAM still shows a local 
max of instability through the forecast area just ahead of what 
may be a triple point in west-central Missouri. Shear is 
extremely strong though and so strong it could rip apart 
updrafts that do try to develop. So it's something to keep an 
eye on but seems lower probability at this point with the 
greater risk for any additional severe weather well southeast of
the forecast area. 

The forecast for the later half of the week and through the weekend 
will feature cooler, closer to normal temperatures. Early next week, 
in the Sunday to Monday time frame, deterministic models show a 
strong shortwave moving through the middle of the country. The GFS, 
in particular, moves very cold air aloft over the region with this 
shortwave. With 925mb temperatures near 0C, this could support some 
wintry precipitation. Only about 15-20% of ensemble clusters show 
anything close to a robust closed system early next week.
