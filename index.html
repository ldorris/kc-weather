Low pressure over central Manitoba is expected to deepen as system 
continues to push east towards the Great Lakes.  Cold front 
associated with this system has pushed into eastern South Dakota 
into central Nebraska by 18Z.  Expect this boundary to continue to 
work south and east, approaching northwest Missouri early this 
evening. Increasing moisture in the vicinity of 850 mb may be 
sufficient for a few showers and storms to develop along the 
boundary this evening.  Instability is fairly limited (generally 500-
1000 J/KG), but increasing speed shear aloft may lead to stronger 
updrafts capable of producing small hail. Low level convergence along 
the front weakens through the evening as flow turns more west to 
east south of the boundary, which should lead to convection becoming 
increasingly sparse through the overnight.  

Orphaned boundary lingers across the forecast area tomorrow.  North 
of the boundary, expect northeast winds while southerly flow remains 
south of the boundary. Cooler airmass to the north will keep 
temperatures in the 60s to lower 70s, while south of the boundary 
temperatures are expected to approach 80 F. There could be enough 
convergence along the front to potentially lead to an isolated late 
day shower/storm along the boundary, but lapse rates are near moist 
adiabatic aloft limiting any severe potential. 

Leading shortwave building east across Kansas tomorrow could lead to 
more scattered shower activity developing across northwestern 
Missouri. A much larger and broad wave lifts north from Texas into 
the Central Plains later on Friday leading to more widespread 
shower/thunderstorm activity Friday night into Saturday.  Halloween 
looks to be fairly gloomy and cooler with scattered showers. 
Precipitable water values 1.1-1.2" (near daily max) should lead to 
fairly efficient rain processes. 

A series of phased waves are expected to build eastward late this 
weekend potentially developing into a cutoff low across the central 
US through at least the firs half of next week.  This could lead to 
cooler more seasonal temperatures and potentially wet
conditions.
