.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 240 PM CDT Fri Oct 23 2026

If you quickly looked at the 500mb analysis between yesterday and 
this morning, it might seem like little has changed. That is 
somewhat accurate across the central CONUS and eastern Rockies. 
The axis of the ridge that dominates the center part of the 
country has shifted a little south and east as the trough across
the western CONUS moves a bit east northeastward. These subtle 
movements are the beginning of an overall pattern shift that is 
expected next week.

For the next few days, above normal temperatures and dry conditions 
continue. Highs will be in the mid to upper 70s with a few spots 
touching 80 degrees. Winds will probably be the most interesting 
weather variable of the day as diurnal mixing will mix swifter 
flowing 850 flow down to the surface; however, the peak of the 850mb 
flow resides over central KS extending into eastern NE and western 
IA; therefore, gusts (around 20-25 MPH) will likely be concentrated 
to areas along and west of the I-35 to I-49 corridors.

As Friday becomes Saturday, a trough continues to progress eastward 
across the western CONUS. Simultaneously a low to midlevel high 
across the eastern seaboard expands westward toward the MO/KS 
border. The end result is a cutoff of midlevel flow yielding a warm 
and dry weekend with less gusty winds.

Deterministic model guidance suggests the southern part of the 
western trough developing into a cut off low over the four corners 
region Sunday. This could further the ongoing storms across that 
region and play a significant role in the weather picture for later 
next week. There is some skepticism as its imminent formation is not 
fully clear on water vapor satellite imagery, but it is more likely 
to manifest itself towards sunrise Saturday. The primary influence 
this system would have is enabling development of showers and storms 
along a synoptic front anticipated to develop from west Texas 
through the northern Great Lakes. This front will be competing 
against the large high across the eastern CONUS mentioned earlier. 
Previous model iterations were more optimistic about precipitation 
making its was eastward late Monday into Tuesday; however, recent 
runs have been trending drier with precipitation chances around 10-
25 percent and those chances only making it to the St Joseph area 
and points northwest. Dry and stable air ahead of the front look to 
win out lowering the chances of rain across the region. However, 
this does not mean the colder air would be held off. Another upper 
level wave pushes a lower level high pressure from interior Canada 
into the central CONUS which will deliver the cooler air. High 
temperatures decrease to the lower 70s before rebounding again 
thanks to some sinusoidal action in the 500mb flow; however, another 
tag team of upper level waves and Canadian air masses look to lower 
temperatures back down toward the upper 60s.

Looking at the extended forecast, guidance suggest further waves 
moving across the region with a slow moving synoptic scale cold 
front expected to drape across the central part of the country toward 
the end of next week. Many models have been using this as a focus 
for shower and storm development; however, at this time scale models 
usually broad brush precipitation potential. Therefore, 
uncertainties linger with precipitation probabilities currently 
sitting around 10-20 percent Thursday and 20-40 percent Friday with 
more fluctuations expected as the forecast updates.
