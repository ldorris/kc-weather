How flags are set (thresholds)

FIRST WARN WEATHER DAY (potentially dangerous; supersedes First Warn): SPC Day 1–3 categorical Slight (2/5)+ over KC · SPC Day 4–8 ≥15% area over KC · snow ≥1" at KCI (NWS gridpoint snowfall, model-median best-fit snow from the winter section, or winter event guidance median) · freezing-rain ice ≥0.01" (NWS gridpoint or model median) · wind chill ≤-10°F or heat index ≥105°F (NWS gridpoint) · any NWS warning (except river/creek Flood Warnings, shown as notes only, and Freeze Warnings, which are First Warn only for plant impacts), flood/flash-flood/severe/winter-storm watch, winter-weather/cold/heat advisory for a DMA point.

FIRST WARN (disruptive, not necessarily dangerous): daytime (6 AM–8 PM CT) NWS gridpoint hourly PoP >50% (beyond the NWS grid or if NWS fails: >50% of models with ≥0.01" daytime precip; commute windows 6–9 AM / 4–7 PM noted) · dense fog: NWS gridpoint visibility ≤0.25 mi or Dense Fog Advisory (model visibility is not in our model feed) · gusts ≥40 mph (NWS gridpoint or model-median peak) · high or daytime feels-like drops ≥15°F day to day (day 1 vs yesterday’s observed at KCI; feels-like = 7 AM–7 PM average) · light wintry mix without accumulation (NWS weather likely+, or ≥half the models) · Wind/Frost/Dense Smoke advisories · Freeze Warning (sensitive plants; not a Weather Day).

.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 122 AM CST Mon Nov 16 2026

H5 height rises will continue today as mid-level ridge axis moves 
eastward. Strong dAVA will continue to support the development of a 
broad surface anticyclone centered over the Great Lakes Region and 
northern Ohio River Valley. The subsidence will continue to provide 
mostly clear skies, perhaps with some high-level cirrus passing 
overhead. Low-level flow turns southeasterly today resulting in 
modest WAA across the region. This will push afternoon highs into 
the mid and upper 50s. Stronger pressure gradient and mixing this 
afternoon may allow for southeastern wind gusts around 15 MPH today. 
This pattern will continue through Tuesday and Wednesday, with the 
main H5 ridge axis passing east of the area early Wednesday morning, 
but providing continued WAA with south to southwesterly lower 
tropospheric flow. Wednesday and Thursday will see temperatures push 
into the mid and upper 60s, with perhaps some lower 70s for areas 
south of U.S. Hwy. 50 on Thursday afternoon.  

Compact short-wave trough with notable vorticity maxima lifts from 
the Rockies into the Northern Plains on Thursday with attendant 
surface cyclone tracking northeastward, and a weak trailing cold 
front. The bulk of the forcing and flow associated with this system 
will miss our forecast area, but if we get enough moisture 
transport, there may be just enough lift to generate some isolated 
shower activity at some point on Thursday into Friday. But current 
ensemble suites keep precipitation probabilities under 20 percent 
for the vast majority of the region as this feature passes through. 

A stronger PV anomaly with deeper mid-level trough and several vort 
maxima will start pushing across the Rockies on Friday and bring 
more forcing into the Plains and lower Missouri Valley by Saturday. 
Deterministic guidance is depicting that the trough will start to 
lift northeastward after entering the High Plains, but even though 
the center of the surface cyclone moves more northeastward, surface 
pressure falls will continue across the area for most of Saturday 
and Sunday, along with better moisture transport ahead of the 
trialing cold front. Rain shower activity is favored over the 
weekend with this system. While the stronger flow will be providing 
plenty of lift and forcing, the instability parameter space remains 
weak both in deterministic and ensemble guidance. Most members of 
the GEFS for example do not bring MUCAPE values any higher than 500 
J/kg. The threat for strong thunderstorms at this point seems 
minimal, though there will a lot of background shear available with 
this feature. Instability trends heading into the weekend will need 
to be monitored. The cold front associated with this system passes 
through Saturday Night and will lead to more seasonable temperatures 
on Sunday and for the start of the next work week.
