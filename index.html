.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 245 PM CDT Thu Oct 22 2026

Ridging at upper levels with complimentary highs at 850mb and the 
surface keep the weather picture consistent for the next 
several days. Southerly flow at the surface continues to push 
warm air northward keeping temperatures above seasonal averages.
NW flow aloft keeps those highs from getting to out of hand, 
but high temperatures are expected to remain in the upper 70s 
to near 80 for most.

The upper level high remains stout across the central CONUS even 
migrating a little northward over the next few days. This slows the 
movements of a trough to the east and sets up the axis of flow west 
of our region keeping passing storm systems to the west and north. 
It is not until Sunday before we expect some significant 
movement in the upper level pattern. A trough across mid 
latitudes of Canada indents the central CONUS high just enough 
to release the cut off low over the Rockies. This combined with
another system moving on shore across the Pacific NW sets up 
the potential for expansive frontogenesis along a line from the
northern Great Lakes through west Texas. As the Pacific NW low 
makes its way further inland it ushers the previous stationary 
low that over the Four Corners region eastward bringing with it chances
for showers and storms Monday evening moreso early Tuesday 
morning. Probabilities for precipitation have been fairly low 
ranging from 10-40 percent extending from the I-35 corridor to 
points northwest. Models have been fairly conservative in 
propagating precipitation chances eastward. Even though 
multiple days of warm air advection would make one think that 
the atmosphere would be primed for convective activity, general 
subsidence from a low to midlevel high across the eastern 
seaboard expanding westward yields a fairly stable environment 
ahead of the low's passing. The question would be if the front 
is able to kinematically force ascent to develop showers and 
storms. Pockets of ascent are likely as upper level CVA 
compliments the low level kinematics of the cold front; 
however, there remains uncertainty how far south and east that 
environment propagates. If rain is able to develop and expand, 
PWAT vales around an inch present opportunities for isolated 
heavy downpours, but not as intense or expansive as we saw about
a month ago.

Looking at the extended, these previously mentioned waves open up 
the pattern fairly significantly. The general delineation 
between the warmer air across the southern CONUS and cooler air
across the north shifts southward opening up avenues for cooler
Canadian air to beginning working its way into the region. This
turns highs back down toward seasonal averages, yet still 
above, around 65-70 degrees (average = 62). It also adds a bit 
more turbulence to the pattern opening up chances for 
additional showers and storms late next week particularly next 
Saturday; although, at this time scale there is a lot that can 
change between now and then.
